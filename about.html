About the terminal

The Talas Civil Air Terminal is the passenger terminal of Irad's only main airfield. It is run by the Civil Aviation Directorate of the Irad Special Economic Zone, which reports to the Ministry of Transport.

History

Talas airfield was built as a Soviet military aerodrome. From 1992 it became Talas Air Base, and between 1993 and 1998 its runway was lengthened to 3,400 m. Civil passengers were first handled in a hangar on the southern apron; the present two-storey terminal was opened in 2001 with Iradiya, founded in 1997, as its main user.

Since 2007 the airfield has been a joint civil–military airfield under arrangements between the ISEZ and the United States; the Joint Use Arrangement of 2016 sets the hours and procedures for civil flights. The base control tower controls all traffic on the airfield, and civil flights are coordinated with the air traffic services of the neighbouring countries.

Civil Aviation Directorate

The Directorate certifies the airline and its crews, runs the civil terminal and the civil aprons, keeps the register of civil aircraft operating in the ISEZ and arranges permits for civil flights to and from Talas. It is part of the Ministry of Transport (Minister Oleg Rudenko). Irad is not a member of the International Civil Aviation Organization.

Photography on the Airfield Road

We remind passengers and those meeting them that photography and filming are forbidden on the Airfield Road, at the South Checkpoint and of the airfield, including from the terminal windows. Cameras and telephones may be checked. Do not stop on the Airfield Road outside the marked places.
